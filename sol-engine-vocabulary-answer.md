# Diagnosis

The candidates introduce metaphors before they explain the mechanic. **Bar** hides the already named resource and never establishes that its units are stacks. **Payoff** and **pays out** hide both the spending ability and its result, while **burns** is a flavorful but less exact substitute for *spends* or *consumes*. **Ember** invents a second name for a unit the game calls a stack. **Feed** indicates growth but not what is growing, and **unleash** does not tell us that the player casts a Demonfire-spending ability. It can also be confused with the named ability Unleash Pestilence [X3].

Two other choices distort the loop. **Strikes** is too narrow because Demonfire also comes from imps, Demon Heart, and being attacked [G]. **Harder it hits** reduces every scaled result to damage, although some spenders gain duration instead [S2-S3]. Even **six** is ungrounded until we call it *six stacks*.

# Vocabulary rule

Let the established system vocabulary carry every mechanical statement: **Demonfire** is the resource, **stacks** are its units, players **build** stacks, abilities **generate** them, spending abilities **spend** Demonfire, and those abilities **consume** the current stacks. Name the result as **damage**, **duration**, or an **additional effect**.

The rule is: use the familiar game verb first and reserve a flavor verb for decoration after the mechanic is clear. We should drop **bar**, **payoff/pays out**, **burn**, and **ember**. We should also drop **feed** and **unleash** from the teaching copy unless the seal keeps those labels and the prose explicitly defines them. We should not use **strikes** as the umbrella term for generators.

# Candidates

## 1. Cleanest default

> Every Knight runs on Demonfire, fed by demons and blood.
> Build Demonfire stacks, then spend them to increase an ability's damage or duration.
> At six stacks, certain abilities trigger an additional effect.

This is the fastest read: the first body sentence gives the loop, and the second explains why six matters.

Trace: **build stacks** [G]; **spend them** and **damage or duration** [S1-S3]; **additional effect at six** [X1-X3].

## 2. Most mechanically explicit

> Every Knight runs on Demonfire, fed by demons and blood.
> Generating abilities build Demonfire; spending abilities consume every stack you have.
> Each stack consumed increases damage or duration; certain abilities gain an added effect at six.

This version clearly separates the two ability categories and states what a spender consumes.

Trace: **generating abilities** [G]; **spending abilities consume the stacks you have** [S1]; **per-stack damage or duration** [S2-S3]; **added effect at six** [X1-X3].

## 3. Most conversational

> Every Knight runs on Demonfire, fed by demons and blood.
> Build Demonfire, then use a spending ability to consume every stack.
> More stacks mean more damage or duration; at six, certain abilities add another effect.

This keeps the exact mechanic but presents it as a simple player action rather than a glossary.

Trace: **build Demonfire** [G]; **spending ability** and **consume every stack** [S1]; **more damage or duration** [S2-S3]; **another effect at six** [X1-X3].

## 4. If FEED / UNLEASH must remain

> Every Knight runs on Demonfire, fed by demons and blood.
> Feed Demonfire by building stacks; unleash it with a spending ability.
> Each stack consumed increases damage or duration; at six, certain abilities add another effect.

This is the strongest version that preserves the seal's flavor labels because it immediately translates both labels into standard game terms.

Trace: **building stacks** [G]; **spending ability** and **each stack consumed** [S1]; **damage or duration** [S2-S3]; **another effect at six** [X1-X3]. The word **unleash** is presentation vocabulary, not a separate mechanical claim.

# Claim lines

**[G] Generation:** Gore, Seeking Flame, and Sear say “Generates 1 Demonfire.” Sever says “Generates 2 stacks of Demonfire.” Shieldgore and the Hellfire Imp also generate two. Demon Heart “instantly generat[es] 6 stacks of Demonfire,” and the defensive passive can “generate a stack of Demonfire when attacked.” These lines support *generate*, *build*, and *stacks* without reducing all generation to strikes.

**[S1] Spending:** The landed spending summary says, “spenders consume the stacks you have; effects scale per stack consumed.” The talent line “Abilities that spend Demonfire” confirms *spend* as the game's own category verb.

**[S2] Damage scaling:** The fiery strike “Deals 20% increased damage per Demonfire consumed.”

**[S3] Duration scaling:** Several self-buffs “Last[] 3 sec for each Demonfire consumed,” and Suffusion of Essence “Increases the duration of Suffuse by 2 sec for each Demonfire consumed.”

**[X1-X3] Six-stack effects:** Flames of Xoroth at six calls down Rain of Chaos [X1]. Skulltaker at six modifies the next Hellmaw [X2]. The supplied six-stack rider for Unleash Pestilence generates Demonfire and grants Infernal Bulwark [X3].

# Seal-label note

Candidates 1 through 3 weaken the literal label match. The ruled lede still supports **FEED** through *fed*, but none of those bodies repeats **UNLEASH**. Candidate 4 preserves both labels, at the cost of keeping the least precise verb in the prose and risking confusion with Unleash Pestilence.

We should change the seal labels instead. **BUILD** on the incoming arc and **SPEND** at the center teach the actual loop in words a WoW player already owns. If the crown pip needs a state label, **6 · BONUS** is more accurate than **6 · UNLEASH**: six adds a rider to certain abilities, but it does not expose one universal unleash action [X1-X3]. With that change, candidate 1 is the strongest body.
