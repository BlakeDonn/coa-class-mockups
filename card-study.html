A · The spec doors

How the class's specs appear on the card. Every door opens the spec profile.

D1 · Name chips

Gains: shortest card; scans as a set. Costs: names alone — a new player learns nothing at the door.

D2 · Spec rows

Gains: each door teaches job and reach before the click. Costs: a taller card; 21 of them lengthen the page.

D3 · Spec rows + verb

Rows plus the authored one-word verb, gloss on tap — the class page's ruled teaching pattern. Gains: the richest door; verbs carry identity. Costs: verbs exist only for Cultist and Tinker today — every other class shows rows without chips until the ×21 authoring pass. Honest, but uneven for a while. Heretic's Convert gloss still carries its research flag.

B · The family row

"Runs in:" with the class's playstyle families, tagline tooltips teaching the jargon.

F1 · Keep the row

Gains: the only place the card says how the class plays, not just what it does; tooltips teach the taxonomy. Costs: family names are jargon on first read.

F2 · Drop it

Gains: a leaner card; families still live in profiles and the starmap. Costs: the card loses its only playstyle signal — roles alone say job, not feel.

C · The tagline slot

The class page's authored tagline, echoed on the card — with the ruled styled-keyword glow.

T1 · No tagline

Gains: uniform cards today. Costs: the card never speaks in the class's voice.

T2 · Authored-only slot

The slot renders only where a tagline is authored; unauthored classes simply omit it — absent, never invented. Gains: the best cards get their voice now; the slot fills as the ×21 authoring lands. Costs: uneven cards until then (one line of height difference).

D · The kick line

The small uppercase line under the class name.

K1 · "Class · 4 specializations"

K2 · Drop it

K1 restates what the doors already show; K2 relies on the doors to carry the count. Small call either way.
